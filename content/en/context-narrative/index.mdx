# Purpose 
When the rate of environmental change exceeds an organisation's ability to make meaning, decisions are made based on a partial/ misinterpreted understanding, which can lead to miscoordination. Continued miscoordination can lead to increasing misalignment between the organisation and its environment of operation, which can risk organisational viability. 

So what does it look like when organisations *can* meaningfully adapt to change?

> The purpose of Prisma is to build the organisational capacity required to make pro-active choices in alignment with collective intentions toward common goals. 

We do this by organising participatory events in which autonomous teams contribute toward the logic that informs how resources are allocated across a self-organising network. We make that traceable, so that all network stakeholders can learn from the choices made. Said another way, we create socio-technical experiments in which participants are able to actively work on aligning the network's economy with increasing orders of ecological health.

---

# Organising
Creating new organisational capabilities requires working in new ways, which may not be immediately intuitive to everyone. In order to be able to work on that at the collective level, prisma aims to create data-backed examples of what those new beginnings look like, through participatory events, as the basis for being able to *learn* from those experiments. Together, these two qualities form the core of how we organise events: action, and learning. 

These events are highly intentional, co-created spaces, built with the purpose of evidencing and publishing this kind of activity. We call those events action-learning journeys. 

> The purpose of action-learning journeys are to evidence systems-change as a step toward increasing orders of organisational capacity. 

---

# Publishing
Publishing is the technical counter-part to the social process of organising events. 

> The purpose of publishing is to **make visible** the process of organising action-learning journeys, which evidence a development in organisational capacity. 

In order to work on making visible the development of new organisational capabilities, we work in terms of **Embedded Development:** Software development which is embedded within the process of event organising. 

This looks like compressed build-and-ship cycles that happen in response to the state of the collective process at any given point in time. By working in this way, as co-development partners, we're able to continually evolve the design requirements, which are being informed on an ongoing basis by the changing context of emergent collective action. 

This approach enables us as an organisation to deliver software development far more aligned with the developmental potential of our partners than we would otherwise be able to, thus creating a higher order of value than shipping software-as-a-service from outside of the context of a participatory event. 

---

# Event Contract
In this way, the process of organising a participatory event becomes a way to create the visibility and viability needed to work collectively on organisational transitions. 

The [event contract](/en/processes/process-infrastructuring/event-contract) becomes the agreement substrate that enables stakeholders to enter into network-scale co-development initiatives, spanning disciplines and organisational boundaries. 

Participatory events play a central role in this system because by committing these initiatives to a timeline, it ensures the associated innovation is grounded in a real-world context. 

Therefore, in conclusion, this system of participatory events aims to offer a way for more people to coordinate around developing the organisational capacity needed for all stakeholders to be able to meaningfully adapt to the changes the rest of this century is likely to bear.
