# Module 2: Testing Hypotheses, Controlling Bias and Rating Signals

Our fieldwork across the **Lagos Hub** (West Africa), **Nairobi Hub** (East Africa) and **Buenos Aires Hub** (Southern Cone) is an exercise in practical market and regulatory testing, not a sales pitch for blockchain. Each interview is designed to test the specific commercial and public-sector workflows in the [Use-Case Hypothesis Register](/en/events/cpc/executing/deliverables/use-case-hypothesis-register) against the payment rails and identity systems that institutions already use every day.

---

## 1. Five rules for unbiased fieldwork

1. **Test the workflow, do not pitch the chain.** Ask how the organisation currently settles cross-border payments, issues credentials or tracks export goods, what that costs in time and fees, and where existing systems fail. Compare any proposed Cardano workflow directly against the domestic incumbent in that country, such as Transferencias 3.0 or QuarkID in Argentina, NIBSS Instant Payments or the National Identification Number in Nigeria, M-Pesa in Kenya, the Pan-African Payment and Settlement System, or USDT on Tron and Polygon.
2. **At least three in four interviewees must be outside the Cardano ecosystem.** In each of our three regions, at least 75% of the people we interview must work in independent institutions such as central banks, commercial payment operators, agricultural cooperatives, universities or trade associations. Interviews using the Cardano Ecosystem Question Pack are capped at 25% of the total.
3. **Go beyond capital-city policy offices.** Pair regulator and central bank interviews in Abuja, Nairobi and Buenos Aires with operational interviews from cooperatives, universities, trade guilds and municipal teams across Lagos, Northern Nigeria and Accra in West Africa, Kigali and Kampala in East Africa, and Montevideo and Asunción in the Southern Cone.
4. **Keep access labels honest.** Every organisation in the [Counterparty Access Map](/en/events/cpc/executing/deliverables/counterparty-access-map) is tracked across five plain categories: Interviewed, Warm Access, Referred, Desk-Identified or Hypothetical. An organisation moves to Interviewed only after an interview has been completed, reviewed in the hub vault and checked by a second hub.
5. **Separate real adoption from talk.** When rating an adoption signal under the Cardano Adoption Signal Standard, never rate a conference announcement, social media post or unfunded memorandum of understanding above the lowest tier (Weak Signal). Only live production usage with recurring transaction volume counts as Tier 1, and only a signed pilot with committed budget and a named institutional owner counts as Tier 2.
