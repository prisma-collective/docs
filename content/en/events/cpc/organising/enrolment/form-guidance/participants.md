# Enrolment Requirements: Regional Hubs and Local Project Teams

This guide explains who can join the CPC fieldwork, what each hub and team is asked to share, what stays private, and how sign-in works once you are approved.

It builds on PRISMA's general [Enrolment](/en/processes/enrolment) process, which exists to build alignment and clarity before any work begins, and on the stage-by-stage [Hub Enrollment Form Design](/en/processes/enrolment/Hub%20Enrollment%20Form%20Design).

---

## 1. Two levels of enrolment

Enrolment happens in two steps, and each step mirrors how trust flows through the project.

**First, PRISMA enrols the three Regional Hubs.** These are the hubs selected at Milestone 2:

- **Lagos Hub** for West Africa, covering Lagos, Abuja, Northern Nigeria and Accra
- **Nairobi Hub** for East Africa, covering Nairobi, Kigali and Kampala
- **Buenos Aires Hub** for the Southern Cone, covering Buenos Aires, Montevideo and Asunción

Once a hub is approved, PRISMA publishes a signed hub registration. The Hub Lead then signs in with the hub's dedicated wallet, which creates the hub's own repository on its data server and links the two identities together.

**Second, each hub enrols its own three Local Project Teams.** That makes nine teams in total. The hub vouches for its teams by publishing their registrations from its own repository.

The network view only accepts a hub registration signed by PRISMA, and only accepts a team registration signed by the hub that team belongs to. Both kinds of registration follow shared templates in the [PRISMA Lexicons](https://github.com/prisma-collective/lexicons).

## 2. Three rules to follow before you enrol

**Use a dedicated wallet for project work.**
Every hub and every team creates a new Cardano wallet used only for this project. Eternl is recommended. Never connect a personal savings wallet or an organisation's main treasury wallet.

The reason is simple. Your wallet becomes your project identity through [Prisma DIDs](/en/processes/enrolment/dids/overview), and that identity is linked to a public research repository. A dedicated wallet keeps your personal transaction history off the public record and keeps project keys separate from anything else you hold. The same wallet later lets PRISMA recognise your verified work with [Verifiable Credentials](/en/processes/enrolment/dids/credential-schemas).

**Nothing becomes public unless you tick its box.**
We ask for everything once, at the start. Answering a question does not make the answer public. Every field that could appear on the public network has its own consent checkbox. Fields without consent, including emails, Telegram handles and phone numbers, stay in private storage and never enter a public repository.

**Complete training before your first interview.**
Every vault reviewer and interviewer completes the five [Interviewer Training Modules](/en/events/cpc/organising/interviewer-training/module-1) and commits to the [stakeholder consent rules](/en/events/cpc/organising/enrolment/form-guidance/local-stakeholders), with Private as the default for every recording.

## 3. Who can enrol

### Regional Hubs

| What we look for | Why it matters | How it is checked |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The hub anchors one of the three selected regions: West Africa, East Africa or the Southern Cone | Phase 3 fieldwork is limited to the regions chosen in Milestone 2 | Checked against the Milestone 2 decisions |
| A named Hub Lead and a named Vault Reviewer | The Hub Lead coordinates the three teams and opens doors locally. The Vault Reviewer approves redacted records within 48 hours of each interview. | Confirmed by the Project Lead, Alfred Itodole |
| Reach into at least four of the seven respondent categories, with at least three in four contacts independent of Cardano | Keeps the evidence balanced | Checked against the [Counterparty Access Map](/en/events/cpc/executing/deliverables/counterparty-access-map) |
| A dedicated hub wallet and access to the Hub Vault review screen | Needed to sign in and approve records | Confirmed during the guided onboarding |

### Local Project Teams

| What we look for | Why it matters | How it is checked |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Vouched for by one of the three hubs, three teams per hub | Keeps accountability local | Approved by the Hub Lead |
| Working in one of the hub's hypothesis areas: H1 to H3 in Lagos, H4 and H5 in Nairobi, H6 and H7 in Buenos Aires | Every interview should test something specific | Matched against the [Use-Case Hypothesis Register](/en/events/cpc/executing/deliverables/use-case-hypothesis-register) |
| Two or three members: a Lead Interviewer, and a Field Scribe who handles uploads and joins the 48-hour review | Splits the work so nothing is missed | Listed in the private team roster |
| A commitment to test ideas honestly, without pitching Cardano | Protects the credibility of the findings | Signed on the enrolment form |
| A dedicated team wallet | Gives the team its own repository and signing key | Checked automatically at enrolment |

## 4. What the forms ask, and what stays private

### Hub form

| Field | Public or private | Public only with consent? |
| :--- | :--- | :-: |
| Hub name | Public | Yes |
| Region (West Africa, East Africa or Southern Cone) | Public | Yes, with the profile |
| Cities and corridors covered | Public | Yes, with the profile |
| Dedicated hub wallet address | Public, as part of the registration | Yes, required to register |
| Confirmation that the wallet is dedicated to the project | Private | No |
| Hub Lead name, email and Telegram | Private, never published | No |
| Vault Reviewer name and contact | Private, never published | No |
| Interview target (15-18 Lagos, 13-16 Nairobi, 14-17 Buenos Aires) | Private, with only the total shown publicly | Yes, total only |
| Consent and ethics commitment | Private | No |

### Team form

| Field | Public or private | Public only with consent? |
| :--- | :--- | :-: |
| Team name or alias | Public | Yes |
| Parent hub | Public | Yes |
| Hypotheses the team is testing | Public | Yes |
| Question packs and interview target (4-6 per team) | Private, with only the total shown publicly | Yes, total only |
| Dedicated team wallet address | Public, as part of the registration | Yes, required to register |
| Confirmation that the wallet is dedicated to the project | Private | No |
| Names and contacts of team members | Private, never published | No |
| Training modules completed | Private | No |
| Consent, ethics and bias commitment | Private | No |

## 5. Approval and sign-in

1. **The hub is approved.** Alfred, as Project Lead, and Tabs, as Principal Investigator, review the hub's form and quota. PRISMA then publishes the hub registration.
2. **The hub signs in (about three minutes).** The Hub Lead connects the dedicated wallet. The hub's repository is created automatically and linked to the wallet, and the hub appears on the network view in [Ground Potentialising](/en/processes/ground-potentialising).
3. **Each team is approved by its hub.** The Hub Lead checks the team's form, hypothesis focus and training, with a quota check from Alfred, and publishes the team registration.
4. **The team signs in (about three minutes).** The Team Lead connects the team wallet and receives a repository on the hub's data server. The team also grants a time-limited signing permission, so it can submit recordings without approving every action in the wallet.
5. **Ready to record.** The team gets access to the Hub Vault intake, through web upload or the Telegram bot, and can start booking Wave 1 interviews.
