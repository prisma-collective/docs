# Desk Research Report, Use-Case Viability Register, and Adoption Signal Assessment

This report presents the Phase 2 desk research findings, comparative systems analysis, use-case hypothesis register, and adoption signal review across all eight candidate clusters, building on the Milestone 1 [Use-Case Hypothesis Register](/en/events/cpc/executing/deliverables/use-case-hypothesis-register), [Adoption Signals (CASS)](/en/events/cpc/executing/terminology/adoption-signals), and [Question Packs](/en/events/cpc/organising/question-packs) .

All protocol telemetry, stablecoin supply figures, statutory citations, and comparative market benchmarks reflect secondary and tertiary evidence captured as of `2026-11-13` (`0 Interviewed`). Each use-case classification (`desk-supported`, `revised`, `contested-weak`, or `rejected-desk`) represents a pre-fieldwork assessment designed for empirical testing and potential falsification during Phase 3 stakeholder interviews (Weeks 7-12).

## 2. Comparative Assessment Against Local Incumbent Systems and Competing Rails

In every regional market evaluated during Phase 2, prospective institutional counterparties already rely on established national payment switches, state identity registries, enterprise resource planning (`ERP`) software, or competing blockchain networks. A public blockchain entry play is viable only where multiple independent organizations must verify shared transactions or credentials across institutional or jurisdictional boundaries without relying on a single trusted database administrator. Where a single central bank switch, telecom operator, or industry association already provides instant, low-cost domestic service, introducing a public ledger adds cost and regulatory complexity without solving an operational problem. ## 3. Phase 2 Use-Case Hypothesis Register (`H1` to `H15`)

In the Milestone 1 [Use-Case Hypothesis Register](/en/events/cpc/executing/deliverables/use-case-hypothesis-register), twelve initial hypotheses (`H1` through `H12`) were logged across seven clusters while leaving desk claims open for Phase 2 investigation. To ensure complete trace coverage across all eight candidate clusters defined in Milestone 1 [Candidate Regions](/en/events/cpc/executing/deliverables/candidate-regions), the Phase 2 register retains `H1` through `H12` verbatim and appends three explicit hypotheses for **Horn / Ethiopia**: **`H13` (Remittances)**, **`H14` (Identity and credentials)**, and **`H15` (Public-service delivery and education records)**.

Prior to Phase 3 fieldwork (`0 Interviewed`), hypotheses are grouped into four provisional desk classifications:

- **`desk-supported` (`4` plays: `H2`, `H5`, `H6`, `H11`):** Secondary evidence documents an acute multi-party problem, an enacted legal pathway, and a verification requirement that is not already solved by a single centralized database. Advances to Phase 3 primary falsification.
- **`revised` (`4` plays: `H1`, `H4`, `H7`, `H12`):** The broad initial framing encounters strong domestic retail switches (`NIBSS NIP`, `M-Pesa`, `Transferencias 3.0`, `PLACSP`), so the hypothesis is narrowed to a specific B2B, cross-border, or sub-threshold audit niche for Phase 3 testing.
- **`contested-weak` (`4` plays: `H3`, `H8`, `H9`, `H10`):** Secondary evidence identifies severe statutory, incumbent, or liquidity barriers. `H3` (Nigeria land registry) is retained for `1-2` diagnostic interviews in West Africa, while `H8` (Brazil), `H9` (Mexico), and `H10` (Andean) are held in the `Monitor` tier without Phase 3 fieldwork allocation.
- **`rejected-desk` (`3` regional plays in Ethiopia, logged alongside the two cross-cutting exclusions in Section 5):** Falsified at the desk screening stage due to active central bank prohibitions (`NBE` crypto ban) and federal standardization on non-blockchain state infrastructure (`Fayda MOSIP`).
