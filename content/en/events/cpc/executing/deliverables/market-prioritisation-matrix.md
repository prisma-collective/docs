# Regional Market Prioritisation Matrix and Infrastructure Prerequisite Assessment

This report presents the Phase 2 regional screening evaluation across the eight candidate country clusters defined in Milestone 1 [Candidate Regions](../milestone-1/candidate-regions.md), applying the ten-dimension Regional Adoption Readiness Framework (RARF) established in Milestone 1 [Adoption Readiness](../milestone-1/research-design-document/adoption-readiness.md) and the advancement criteria set out in Milestone 1 [Region Selection](../milestone-1/research-design-document/region-selection.md) (`REF-72`). East Asia remains outside the scope of this study, as established in Milestone 1.

All ratings in this matrix represent provisional desk estimates based on secondary and tertiary evidence captured as of `2026-11-13`, prior to Phase 3 stakeholder interviews (`0 Interviewed`). Every score, advancement classification, and infrastructure status is designed for empirical testing and potential falsification during Phase 3 primary fieldwork (Weeks 7-12).

---

## 1. Methodological Approach and Desk-Stage Calibration

Secondary research can verify enacted legislation, central bank circulars, domestic payment-switch volume, and public on-chain liquidity. Secondary data cannot verify whether a specific agency or enterprise holds active budget authority for a pilot, whether a local systems integrator can sustain production service levels, or how quickly a compliance department will clear a new settlement rail. To reflect this evidentiary boundary prior to primary interviews, the Phase 2 evaluation applies four methodological conventions across all 80 cells of the RARF matrix:

1. **Distinction Between Secondary Evidence and Primary Confirmation:** On the 1-to-5 RARF rubric, top-tier readiness (`5`) requires direct confirmation of operational commitment and verified institutional capacity. Because Phase 2 evaluates desk evidence prior to stakeholder interviews, dimensions measurable through official statistics and statutory texts (`D1` Problem Severity, `D2` Use-Case Fit, `D4` Regulatory Readiness, `D5` Infrastructure Readiness, `D6` Stablecoin and Settlement Readiness, and `D8` Existing Ecosystem Presence) reach up to `4` where secondary evidence is strong, while `5` is reserved for post-fieldwork evaluation in Milestone 4.
2. **Conservative Scoring on Fieldwork-Dependent Dimensions (`D3`, `D7`, `D9`, `D10`):** Four dimensions depend directly on primary field validation:
   - **Counterparty Accessibility (`D3`):** Rated up to `3` at the desk stage. While the research team maintains active institutional networks in Buenos Aires, Lagos, Nairobi, and Barcelona (`Tier 2: Warm Access` and `Tier 3: Referred`), those relationships remain pre-interview recruitment channels until primary sessions are completed.
   - **Delivery Capacity (`D7`):** Rated up to `3` pending technical assessment of local software cooperatives and systems integrators using the [Delivery Partners Question Pack](../milestone-1/research-design-document/question-packs/delivery-partners.md).
   - **Adoption Signal Strength (`D9`):** Rated up to `3` in accordance with the Milestone 1 [Adoption Signals](../milestone-1/research-design-document/adoption-signals.md) standard, under which `CASS Tier 1` (Verified Adoption) and `CASS Tier 2` (Strong Signal) require primary confirmation of active institutional ownership and resource allocation.
   - **Time-to-Pilot Feasibility (`D10`):** Rated up to `3` pending primary verification of procurement timelines and compliance sign-off procedures.
3. **Weighting Schedule and Desk Ceiling:** Under the standard RARF weighting schedule (`D1: 15%`, `D2: 15%`, `D3: 15%`, `D4: 10%`, `D5: 10%`, `D6: 10%`, `D7: 10%`, `D8: 5%`, `D9: 5%`, `D10: 5%`), the conservative desk-stage calibration produces a maximum theoretical composite score of `3.65 / 5.00` (`73.0 / 100`) prior to fieldwork. Both the weighted composite score and the unweighted ten-dimension mean are reported in Table 1 so readers can compare rankings across both specifications.
4. **Advancement Classifications and Fieldwork Allocation:**
   - **`Conditional-Go` (`3.20 - 3.99`):** Satisfies all five Milestone 1 advancement criteria (scores of `3.0` or higher on at least six of the ten dimensions, an identifiable 12-24 month counterparty pathway, a specific testable use case, independent problem-severity data, and no blocking regulatory or infrastructure constraint). Advances to Phase 3 primary research.
   - **`Monitor` (`2.50 - 3.19`):** Displays meaningful market scale or specific strengths, but encounters domestic incumbent saturation, high statutory capital thresholds, regulatory licensing gaps, or the absence of a local field hub. Retained in the comparative baseline without Phase 3 interview allocation.
   - **`No-Go` (below `2.50`):** Faces an active central bank or statutory prohibition that precludes compliant pilot implementation within 12-24 months. Excluded from Phase 3 fieldwork.
   - Following the resource-allocation model defined in Milestone 1 [Region Selection](../milestone-1/research-design-document/region-selection.md), roughly `60%` of Phase 3 interview capacity (`26-34` of `42-52` planned target interactions, or `61.5%-65.4%`) is assigned to the top two regional clusters (**Southern Cone** via **Buenos Aires Hub** and **West Africa** via **Lagos Hub**), while the remaining `~40%` (`16-18` target interactions) covers the third priority emerging-market cluster (**East Africa** via **Nairobi Hub**, `12-15` interviews) and the comparative European regulatory benchmark in **Iberia** (**Barcelona**, `3-4` interviews).

## 4. Regional Market Narratives

### 4.1 Southern Cone (`Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay`) - `3.40 / 5.00` (`Conditional-Go`, Priority Playbook 1)

Argentina records the highest stablecoin transaction share in Latin America, exceeding `60%` of national crypto volume (`REF-56`) due to chronic peso inflation and multi-tier foreign-exchange spreads (`REF-37`). Over the past two years, the legal environment for virtual-asset commerce in the region has matured substantially. In Argentina, Executive Decree `DNU 70/2023` amended Articles 765 and 766 of the Civil and Commercial Code (`REF-32`), allowing private parties to denominate and settle commercial obligations in any foreign currency or virtual asset. Simultaneously, Law `27.739` and National Securities Commission (`CNV`) General Resolution `1058/2025` (`REF-31`) established a formal prudential and custody regime for more than 100 registered Virtual Asset Service Providers (`PSAVs`), while Uruguay enacted Law `20.345` in September 2024 (`REF-39`) to place virtual-asset providers under Central Bank (`BCU/SSF`) supervision. The primary regulatory constraint in Argentina lies in the banking perimeter: Central Bank (`BCRA`) Communications `"A" 7506` and `"A" 7759` (`REF-33`) prohibit commercial banks and retail payment service providers (`PSPs`) from offering crypto custody on their own balance sheets. As a result, registered PSAVs (`Lemon Cash`, `Belo`, `Ripio`, `Fiwind`) rely on licensed third-party banking APIs (`BIND`) to bridge virtual assets into the national `Transferencias 3.0` interoperable QR network (`CVU`/`CBU`).

At the municipal and institutional layer, the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires (`GCBA`) operates Latin America's largest public self-sovereign identity deployment (`QuarkID` / `miBA`, serving more than `3.6M` citizens, `REF-36`), alongside active university (`UBA`, `UTN`) and technology cooperative (`FACTTIC`, `REF-38`) ecosystems. However, incumbent competition is well established: `QuarkID` currently anchors its primary state hashes on `ZKsync Era` (`Ethereum L2`), and consumer stablecoin cards in Argentina settle overwhelmingly on `Tron TRC-20` and `Polygon`. Because domestic peso retail payments are already served by `Transferencias 3.0` and `Mercado Pago`, Phase 3 fieldwork anchored by the **Buenos Aires Hub** (`12-16` planned interviews) will focus on two multi-party niches where existing domestic rails leave friction: B2B cooperative and agribusiness programmable USD contract escrow under Article 765 CCyC (`H6`), and cross-border Mercosur (`AR-UY-PY`) professional credential verification and contractor settlement (`H7`). Primary interviews will test whether registered Argentine PSAVs will integrate Cardano `USDCx` or `USDM` settlement APIs and whether `QuarkID` maintainers will support `did:prism` (`Hyperledger Identus` / `Veridian`) or `did:plc` DID resolution alongside `ZKsync`.

### 4.2 West Africa (`Ghana, Nigeria, Senegal`) - `3.25 / 5.00` (`Conditional-Go`, Priority Playbook 2)

Nigeria and Ghana anchor Sub-Saharan Africa's largest digital-asset and remittance corridor, generating significant share of the region's `USD 205B` annual on-chain volume (`REF-56`) against formal cross-border remittance costs of `7.9%` to `8.4%` (`REF-55`). Regulatory clarity in both countries improved markedly between December 2023 and late 2025. In Nigeria, Central Bank (`CBN`) Circular `FPR/DIR/PUB/CIR/002/003` (`REF-03`) lifted the 2021 banking ban on VASP settlement accounts, while the Investments and Securities Act (`ISA`) 2025 (`REF-01`) and the SEC's Accelerated Regulatory Incubation Program (`ARIP`, `REF-02`) established statutory licensing pathways for local exchanges such as `Busha` and `Quidax`. In parallel, Ghana enacted its Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025 (`Act 1154`, `REF-12`), formalizing joint Bank of Ghana (`BoG`) and SEC oversight. By contrast, Senegal operates within the restrictive `BCEAO` (WAEMU) banking union (`REF-15`) and is retained strictly as a secondary desk sub-market.

A realistic assessment of West Africa must account for the strength of domestic Web2 infrastructure and competing blockchain networks. For local-currency retail transfers, Nigeria's instant interbank switch (`NIBSS NIP`, processing over `9B` annual transactions, `REF-10`) and Ghana's `GhIPSS GIP` and `MTN MoMo` (`REF-14`) already provide near-instant settlement, while `Tron TRC-20 USDT` and `Celo MiniPay` (`over 5M` activations, `REF-58`) dominate retail dollar savings. At the same time, Nigeria's regulated Naira stablecoin (`cNGN`, `REF-11`) currently maintains zero supply on Cardano (`REF-59`), and CBN rules mandate Naira-only payouts for retail international money transfer operators (`IMTOs`, `REF-04`). In the identity and public-records domains, single-authority verification is already served by centralized national systems, including `NIMC`'s National Identification Number (`over 115M` enrolled) accessed via licensed Verification Service Providers (`Smile ID`, `Seamfix`, `Prembly`, `REF-09`) and the Nigerian Insurers Association's centralized `NIID` database (`askNIID` / `*565*11#`, `REF-10`). Similarly, statutory land transfers under Nigeria's Land Use Act 1978 (`Section 22`, `REF-07`) require discretionary Governor's Consent inside state-controlled GIS databases (`Lagos e-GIS`, `Kaduna KADGIS`). Anchored by the **Lagos Hub** (`14-18` planned interviews across Lagos, Abuja, Northern Nigeria, and Accra), Phase 3 fieldwork will therefore test wholesale B2B and inter-MTO cross-border liquidity (`H1`), multi-issuer academic and trade-guild verifiable credentials that cross institutional boundaries (`H2`), and a targeted `1-2` interview diagnostic check on whether state land records (`H3`) face insurmountable statutory lock-in.

### 4.3 East Africa (`Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda`) - `3.20 / 5.00` (`Conditional-Go`, Priority Playbook 3)

East Africa pairs high mobile-money maturity with two structural external demand drivers: European Union export compliance requirements for smallholder agriculture and persistent `5.5%` to `8.0%` cross-border East African Community (`EAC`) remittance costs (`REF-55`). On the regulatory front, Kenya's Virtual Asset Service Providers (`VASP`) Act, 2025 entered into force on `2025-11-04` (`REF-16`), creating a bifurcated licensing framework administered by the Central Bank of Kenya (`CBK`) for payment and custody rails and the Capital Markets Authority (`CMA`) for exchanges and tokenization. Equally important for commercial unit economics, Kenya's Finance Act 2025 (`REF-17`) repealed the punitive `3%` Digital Asset Tax on gross transaction principal, replacing it with a `10%` excise duty levied solely on VASP platform service fees. Rwanda (`RURA`/`BNR`) and Uganda (`FIA`/`BoU`) provide supportive secondary markets for regional trade and agricultural pilots (`REF-23`, `REF-24`).

Within Kenya, Safaricom's `M-Pesa` (`over 34M` active users and `over 96%` adult penetration, `REF-20`) and the government's `eCitizen` portal (`over 13M` users, `REF-21`) thoroughly address domestic retail payments and central government fee collection, leaving no viable case for a blockchain replacement of domestic Kenyan shilling retail rails. However, significant multi-party verification gaps persist in agricultural export supply chains (`H5`) and cross-border or humanitarian cash-based transfers (`H4`). Under the EU Deforestation Regulation (`EUDR`, Regulation (EU) 2023/1115, `REF-22`), European coffee, cocoa, and timber buyers must verify farm-plot polygon geo-coordinates (`GeoJSON`) and deforestation-free custody chains across hundreds of independent primary cooperatives, millers, and exporters. While agritech ERPs (`iProcure`, `Apollo Agriculture`, `KTDA`) manage internal cooperative data in SQL databases, European importers and auditors require tamper-evident cross-actor provenance proofs. In parallel, local payment aggregators such as `Kotani Pay` (`REF-25`) already bridge stablecoins into `M-Pesa` over USSD (`*483#`), though over `95%` of current commercial volume settles on `Celo`, `Tron`, `Polygon`, and `Stellar`. Phase 3 fieldwork led by the **Nairobi Hub** (`12-15` planned interviews) will test whether export cooperatives and agritech integrators will adopt `Hyperledger Identus` credentials and `CIP-0020` batch lot anchoring (hosted on Kenyan servers to comply with Section 40 of the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019, `REF-18`), and what liquidity incentives are required to activate Cardano `USDCx`/`USDM` settlement on Kenyan USSD/M-Pesa ramps.

### 4.4 Iberia (`Spain - Barcelona`, Applicant-Justified) - `3.20 / 5.00` (`Conditional-Go: Comparative EU Benchmark`)

Spain exhibits low domestic payment friction (`D1 = 2`) because `SEPA Instant` and the interbank mobile switch `Bizum` (`over 27M` users, `REF-54`) provide instant, zero-fee euro transfers. Iberia's value to the research portfolio lies instead in its high regulatory and institutional clarity (`D2 = 4`, `D4 = 4`, `D5 = 4`) under the European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets (`MiCA`) Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 (`REF-49`) and the eIDAS 2.0 Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 (`REF-50`), which mandates public-sector recognition of Electronic Attestations of Attributes (`EAAs` / verifiable credentials). At the municipal procurement layer, Article 118 of Spain's Public Sector Contracts Law (`LCSP Ley 9/2017`, `REF-53`) allows municipal agencies, public foundations, and universities to award direct minor service contracts (`Contratos Menores`) under `EUR 15,000` (excluding VAT) without an open EU tender. At the same time, Spain's Real Decreto-ley `14/2019` (`REF-51`) prohibits public administrations from using distributed ledgers for statutory citizen identification until special state security regulations are enacted, and European Data Protection Board (`EDPB`) Guidelines `02/2025` (`REF-52`) strictly forbid placing personal data or unsalted personal identifiers on public blockchains.

Because statutory public tenders are locked into centralized state portals (`PLACSP` and Catalonia's `PSCP`) and statutory citizen identity is governed by `Cl@ve`, `DNIe`, and Catalonia's `idCAT` (`AOC`), Iberia advances strictly as a bounded **Comparative European Regulatory and Civic Benchmark (`3-4` targeted interviews in Barcelona)** rather than displacing any of the three emerging-market playbooks. Drawing on the research team's institutional access to Barcelona's open-source civic-tech ecosystem (`Decidim`, used by more than `400` municipalities globally, `REF-54`) and Catalan cooperative federations (`Coopolis`), Phase 3 benchmark interviews will test two specific questions: whether universities and cooperative networks can pilot off-chain `Veridian` / `Hyperledger Identus` verifiable credentials (`H11`) under `eIDAS 2.0` and `LCSP Art. 118`, and whether federated grant and participatory-budgeting programmes on `Decidim` (`H12`) see institutional value in anchoring tamper-evident content hashes (`strongRef` + `CIP-0020` batch metadata) on Cardano while keeping all personal data off-chain.

### 4.5 Non-Advancing Clusters Held on `Monitor` (`Brazil`, `Andean`, `Mexico`) and `No-Go` (`Horn / Ethiopia`)

Four candidate clusters do not advance to Phase 3 fieldwork, though three (`Brazil`, `Andean`, and `Mexico`) remain in the comparative `Monitor` register while `Horn / Ethiopia` is classified as `No-Go`:

- **Brazil (`3.05 / 5.00` - `Monitor`):** Brazil possesses Latin America's largest institutional financial market and enacted virtual-asset statute Law `14.478/2022`. However, two structural developments in 2025-2026 limit near-term Cardano entry. First, the Central Bank of Brazil's (`BCB`) instant payment system **`Pix`** (`over 175M` users, `REF-42`) completely satisfies domestic retail payment demand, and BCB's wholesale **`Drex`** initiative pivoted away from distributed ledger technology in late 2025 (`REF-42`). Second, **BCB Resolutions `519, 520, and 521/2025`** (effective `2026-02-02`, `REF-41`) classify international stablecoin transfers as regulated Foreign Exchange (`Cambio`) operations, restricts transactions with unhosted wallets, and imposes `BRL 2M to BRL 14M` (`~USD 350k-2.5M`) minimum capital requirements on licensed `SPSAVs`. Institutional BRL stablecoins (`Braza BBRL` and `Transfero BRZ`, `REF-43`) operate on `Polygon`, `Solana`, `Tron`, and `Ethereum`, while Cardano holds `USD 0.00` in BRL stablecoin liquidity (`REF-59`) and the 2023-2025 Petrobras engagement was restricted to employee training workshops (`REF-67`). Combined with the absence of a funded local field hub (`D3 = 2`), Brazil is held on `Monitor`.
- **Andean (`Colombia, Peru` - `2.80 / 5.00` - `Monitor`):** In both Peru and Colombia, domestic mobile payment switches (`Yape` and `Plin` with BCRP-mandated QR interoperability in Peru, `REF-48`; `Nequi`, `DaviPlata`, and the central bank's `Bre-B` instant rail in Colombia, `REF-46`, `REF-47`) cover retail local-currency transfers, while dollar savings apps (`Littio`) run on `Polygon` and `Avalanche`. Colombia remains in a statutory VASP licensing gap following the expiration of the Financial Superintendency's (`SFC`) `LaArenera` pilot sandbox (`REF-46`), and Peru's SBS Resolution `02648-2024` (`REF-48`) is limited to AML/Travel Rule registration. With no local field hub in Bogota or Lima (`D3 = 2`), the Andean cluster is held on `Monitor`.
- **Mexico (`2.75 / 5.00` - `Monitor`):** Although the US-Mexico remittance corridor exceeds `USD 64.7B` annually (`REF-45`), **`Bitso`** already processes more than `USD 8B` per year (`~10%+` of the entire national corridor) using `XRPL`, `USDC`, and `USDT` directly into **`Banxico SPEI`** 24/7 instant interbank rails (`REF-45`). At the same time, **Banxico Circular `4/2019`** (`REF-44`) prohibits regulated Mexican financial technology institutions (`ITFs`) and commercial banks from offering client-facing virtual-asset services on regulated balance sheets, forcing operators into complex dual-entity corporate structures (`IFPE` + `LFPIORPI Art. 17 XVI`). Because Cardano's total global stablecoin supply (`USD 60.4M`, `REF-59`) represents less than `1%` of Bitso's annual corridor throughput and the team has no local field hub in Mexico (`D3 = 2`), Mexico scores `>= 3.0` on only `4 of 10` RARF dimensions and is held on `Monitor`.
- **Horn / Ethiopia (`2.05 / 5.00` - `No-Go`):** Despite severe foreign-exchange scarcity (`D1 = 4`), the **National Bank of Ethiopia (`NBE`) Public Notice of May 2022** and **Proclamation `No. 1282/2023`** (`REF-26`) explicitly prohibit cryptocurrency payments and virtual-asset settlement (`D2 = 1`, `D6 = 1`), and the July 2024 Birr float (**Directive `FXD/01/2024`**, `REF-27`) restricts formal remittance termination to NBE-licensed banks, `EthSwitch`, and state-backed `Telebirr` (`over 54M` users, `REF-30`). In the digital identity and education sector (`H14`, `H15`), the Federal Government enacted **Proclamation `No. 1284/2023`** (`REF-28`) and partnered with the World Bank (`USD 350M` Project `P179040`) to deploy **`Fayda`** on the open-source, non-blockchain **`MOSIP`** platform (`over 13M-36M` enrolled), superseding the stalled 2021 Ministry of Education `Atala PRISM` announcement (`REF-67`). In addition, **Proclamation `No. 1321/2024`** and **INSA Proclamation `No. 808/2013`** (`REF-29`) mandate sovereign local server hosting and cryptographic security approval. Ethiopia is therefore classified as `No-Go` for near-term operational entry and retained as an empirical case study on sovereign platform competition.
