# Preliminary Regulatory and Compliance Scan

This report examines the statutory, regulatory, data protection, foreign-exchange, and public-procurement environments across all eight candidate regional clusters evaluated during Phase 2 desk research (`REF-72`), establishing the legal baseline for Phase 3 stakeholder interviews using the Milestone 1 [Regulators and Policy Question Pack](../milestone-1/research-design-document/question-packs/regulators.md).

All enacted statutes, central bank circulars, tax reforms, and regulatory sandbox statuses reflect secondary and tertiary legal evidence captured as of `2026-11-13` (`0 Interviewed`).

---

## 1. Scope, Analytical Framework, and Legal Notice

> **Regulatory Scope Notice:** This study is a comparative regulatory, statutory, and public-procurement workability assessment compiled during Phase 2 desk research. It does not constitute formal legal advice or a formal legal opinion in any jurisdiction. In accordance with Milestone 1 [Decision Gates](../milestone-1/research-design-document/decision-gates.md), each regional cluster and candidate use case carries a paired **Regulatory Certainty Rating** (`Clear (High Certainty)`, `Evolving (Medium Certainty)`, or `Uncertain (Low Certainty)`) and a **`Specialist / Local Counsel Required`** indicator specifying the exact statutory questions that require local legal counsel sign-off prior to any institutional pilot or capital deployment.

Each candidate cluster is evaluated across seven statutory and administrative dimensions:

1. **Competent Authorities and Enacted Statutes:** Central banks, securities commissions, financial intelligence units, data protection authorities, and public procurement bodies, together with enacted laws and circulars in force as of `2026-11-13`.
2. **Licensing, Registration, and Sandbox Regimes:** Operational statutory licensing frameworks, active regulatory incubation cohorts, and expired or non-operational sandbox programmes.
3. **Stablecoin and Virtual-Asset Payment Posture:** Classification as `Permitted`, `Regulated`, `Restricted`, `Prohibited`, or `Unclear` across both retail payments and B2B institutional settlement.
4. **Data Protection, Privacy, and Public-Ledger Rules:** Interaction between immutable on-chain state and statutory data-subject rights, including rectification, erasure, suppression, and domestic server localisation mandates.
5. **Public Procurement Rules, Contracting Thresholds, and Title Constraints:** Statutory tender requirements, direct minor-contract ceilings (`Contratos Menores` and low-value innovation thresholds), domestic equity rules, and executive title gates.
6. **Foreign Exchange (`FX`) Controls and Capital Rules:** Central bank FX matching rules, parallel-market spreads, Travel Rule thresholds, and cross-border settlement perimeters.
7. **Paired Regulatory Certainty Rating and Local Counsel Verification:** Explicit distinction between statutory clarity (how clearly the rules are written) and commercial workability (the capital and compliance cost required to operate within those rules).

## 3. Regional Regulatory Narratives

### 3.1 Southern Cone (`Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay` - `Buenos Aires Hub`)

Argentina's legal framework combines commercial contract freedom with a strict regulatory perimeter between registered virtual-asset providers and the commercial banking system. Under Executive Decree `DNU 70/2023`, which amended Articles 765 and 766 of the Argentine Civil and Commercial Code (`CCyC`, `REF-32`), private parties may lawfully denominate and settle commercial obligations in any foreign currency or virtual asset, removing the former statutory rule that allowed debtors to force conversion into Argentine Pesos (`ARS`) at official exchange rates. This reform provides a clear legal basis for B2B cooperative invoice and escrow settlement (`H6`, `H7`). On the supervisory side, Law `27.739` (enacted March 2024) and National Securities Commission (`CNV`) General Resolution `1058/2025` (enacted March 2025, `REF-31`) established a mandatory registry and prudential rulebook for Virtual Asset Service Providers (`PSAVs`) operating in Argentina above `USD 35,000` in monthly volume. Registered PSAVs must maintain minimum net-worth thresholds (`USD 35,000` to `USD 150,000` depending on category), segregate client assets, pass annual cybersecurity audits, and comply with Financial Information Unit (`UIF`) Resolution `49/2024` Travel Rule reporting (`REF-34`). However, Central Bank (`BCRA`) Communications `"A" 7506` and `"A" 7759` (`REF-33`) prohibit commercial banks and retail Payment Service Providers (`PSPs` offering `CVU` virtual accounts) from holding or offering virtual assets on their regulated balance sheets. Consequently, Argentine crypto platforms (`Lemon Cash`, `Belo`, `Ripio`, `Fiwind`) operate dual-entity structures in which a `CNV`-registered `PSAV` manages virtual-asset custody and connects via API to a licensed bank bridge (`Banco Industrial / BIND`, `Coelsa`) for `Transferencias 3.0` peso settlement. Any Cardano stablecoin pilot in Argentina must therefore integrate with an existing `CNV`-registered `PSAV` rather than pitching direct balance-sheet custody to a commercial bank.

Across the Rio de la Plata, Uruguay enacted Law `20.345` in September 2024 (`REF-39`), amending the Central Bank Charter (`Law 16.696`) to create a dedicated `PSAV` supervisory regime under the Superintendency of Financial Services (`SSF`) of the Central Bank of Uruguay (`BCU`). Coupled with free foreign-exchange convertibility and European Union `GDPR`-adequate data protection under Law `18.331` (matching Argentina's `Law 25.326` `Habeas Data` protections, `REF-35`), Uruguay offers a highly compatible bi-national legal counterparty for Mercosur cross-border contractor and credential pilots (`H7`). Paraguay complements the cluster with active anti-money-laundering registration under `SEPRELAD Resolution 313/2021` and `24/7` `SIPAP` interbank settlement (`REF-40`).

### 3.2 West Africa (`Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal` - `Lagos Hub`)

West Africa's two largest Anglophone markets transitioned from regulatory prohibition to structured virtual-asset licensing between late 2023 and the end of 2025. In Nigeria, the Central Bank (`CBN`) issued Circular `FPR/DIR/PUB/CIR/002/003` on 22 December 2023 (`REF-03`), lifting its February 2021 prohibition on commercial bank accounts for crypto entities and authorizing banks to maintain designated settlement accounts for licensed Virtual Asset Service Providers (`VASPs`) while continuing to bar banks from trading virtual assets on their own balance sheets. In March 2025, Nigeria enacted the Investments and Securities Act 2025 (`ISA 2025`, `REF-01`), codifying virtual and digital assets under the statutory jurisdiction of the Securities and Exchange Commission (`SEC Nigeria`). Through its Accelerated Regulatory Incubation Program (`ARIP`, `REF-02`), `SEC Nigeria` granted provisional operating approvals to domestic exchanges including `Busha` and `Quidax` alongside the `cNGN` consortium (`REF-11`). At the same time, CBN Circular `TED/FEM/PUB/FPC/001/003` and the Electronic Foreign Exchange Matching System (`EFEMS`, `REF-04`) strictly regulate cross-border foreign-exchange pricing and mandate Naira-only payouts for retail international money transfer operators (`IMTOs`). In Ghana, Parliament enacted the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act in December 2025 (`Act 1154`, `REF-12`), converting the Bank of Ghana (`BoG`) regulatory sandbox into a permanent dual-licensing regime shared between `BoG` (payments and custody) and `SEC Ghana` (exchange and issuance). By contrast, Senegal remains subject to `BCEAO` (`WAEMU`) banking instructions that bar regional financial institutions from virtual-asset operations (`REF-15`), confining Phase 3 West African pilot design to the Nigeria-Ghana corridor.

For public-sector and credential use cases in Nigeria (`H2`, `H3`), three non-financial statutes govern pilot feasibility. First, Sections 24-38 and 41-43 of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (`NDPA 2023`, `REF-06`) enforce data-subject erasure rights and cross-border transfer restrictions on sensitive personal data (`NIMC NIN`, `REF-09`), requiring that verifiable credentials remain off-chain inside user wallets or encrypted cloud agents (`Hyperledger Identus` / `Veridian`). Second, the National Information Technology Development Agency (`NITDA`) requires prior technical approval under its IT Projects Clearance Guidelines (`REF-05`) before federal ministries, departments, or agencies can execute technology procurement contracts under the Public Procurement Act 2007 (`REF-08`). Third, for land administration (`H3`), Section 22 of the Nigerian Land Use Act 1978 (`Cap L5 LFN 2004`, `REF-07`) makes it unlawful to alienate any statutory right of occupancy without the prior discretionary consent of the State Governor. Because legal title validity depends on executive sign-off recorded in state-controlled GIS registries (`Lagos e-GIS`, `Kaduna KADGIS`), an external public blockchain cannot confer statutory title on its own.

### 3.3 East Africa (`Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda` - `Nairobi Hub`)

Kenya enacted two landmark legislative reforms in 2025 that fundamentally reshaped its regulatory environment for digital assets. First, the Virtual Asset Service Providers (`VASP`) Act, 2025 received Presidential assent in October 2025 and entered into force on `4 November 2025` (`REF-16`), replacing a decade of central bank caution with a formal statutory licensing regime. Under the Act, the Central Bank of Kenya (`CBK`) supervises virtual-asset payment processing, stablecoin custody, and fiat settlement gateways, while the Capital Markets Authority (`CMA`) regulates exchanges, brokers, and token issuance. Second, the Finance Act 2025 (`REF-17`) repealed the punitive `3%` Digital Asset Tax (`DAT`) previously charged on gross transaction volume (which had exceeded institutional market-maker spreads) and replaced it with a `10%` excise duty levied solely on VASP platform service commissions. Across the East African Community (`EAC`), Rwanda operates an active regulatory sandbox under the National Bank of Rwanda (`BNR`) and `CMA Rwanda` while advancing its draft VASP framework (`REF-23`), and Uganda permits bounded testing inside the Bank of Uganda (`BoU`) National Payment Systems Act 2020 sandbox alongside Financial Intelligence Authority (`FIA`) AML registration (`REF-24`).

Any credential or agricultural traceability deployment in Kenya (`H4`, `H5`) must also satisfy strict statutory data-localisation and public-procurement rules. Section 40 of the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 and the Data Protection (General) Regulations 2021 (`REF-18`), enforced by the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (`ODPC`), require data controllers processing personal data for public-interest, civil-registration, or revenue systems to maintain at least one serving copy of personal data on a server physically located in Kenya, and prohibit writing unencrypted personal data to external public blockchains. Consequently, for agricultural export traceability under the EU Deforestation Regulation (`EUDR`, Regulation (EU) 2023/1115, `REF-22`), farmer identity and plot records must be hosted on Kenyan servers (`Identus` agent or cooperative ERP), anchoring only anonymised batch hashes and polygon commitments on Cardano. Similarly, because Sections 155-157 of the Kenya Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Act (`PPADA 2015`, `REF-19`) impose a `40%` local shareholding requirement and multi-quarter tender cycles on central government procurement, Phase 3 fieldwork focuses on private agricultural export cooperatives (`H5`) and humanitarian NGO programmes (`H4`) that contract directly outside state tender delays.

### 3.4 Iberia (`Spain - Barcelona`, Comparative EU Benchmark)

Spain operates under the European Union's fully codified digital-asset and digital-identity statutes. Spain's transitional window under the Markets in Crypto-Assets (`MiCA`) Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 closed on `30 December 2025` (`REF-49`), requiring Crypto-Asset Service Providers (`CASPs`) to hold authorization from the National Securities Market Commission (`CNMV`) or an EU home regulator, and restricting regulated stablecoin services to EU-authorized E-Money Tokens (`EMTs` such as `Circle EURC`). However, the two hypotheses evaluated in the Barcelona benchmark (`H11: Cooperative and Academic Verifiable Credentials` and `H12: Participatory Budgeting and Grant Auditability`) are non-financial, non-custodial credential and audit-log workflows that do not involve issuing or brokering crypto-assets under `MiCA`.

Instead, public-adjacent credential and audit pilots in Spain are governed by three specific statutes. First, the European Union's `eIDAS 2.0` Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 (`REF-50`) establishes mandatory legal recognition of Electronic Attestations of Attributes (`EAAs` / verifiable credentials) and `EUDI Wallets`. Second, Spain's Real Decreto-ley `14/2019` (`amending Ley 39/2015 Art. 9.2(c)`, `REF-51`) prohibits Spanish public administrations from using distributed ledger systems for statutory citizen identification until special national security regulations are enacted, while European Data Protection Board (`EDPB`) Guidelines `02/2025` (`REF-52`) warn that writing even hashed personal identifiers to a public blockchain violates `GDPR Article 17` if the hash can be linked back to an identifiable natural person. Accordingly, Barcelona benchmark architectures (`Veridian` / `Hyperledger Identus` and `Decidim`) must keep 100% of personal data off-chain and use Cardano strictly to anchor institutional issuer registries or anonymous batch content hashes (`strongRef`). Third, on the procurement side, Article 118 of Spain's Public Sector Contracts Law (`LCSP Ley 9/2017`, `REF-53`) allows municipal innovation foundations (`Bit Habitat`), public universities, and consortia to award direct minor service contracts (`Contratos Menores`) below `EUR 15,000` (excluding VAT) for up to 12 months without an open EU tender.

### 3.5 Non-Advancing Clusters (`Brazil, Andean, Mexico, Horn / Ethiopia`)

- **Brazil (`Monitor - 3.05 / 5.00`, `RARF D4 = 3`):** Enacted in November 2025 and effective `2 February 2026`, Central Bank of Brazil (`BCB`) Resolutions `519, 520, and 521/2025` (`REF-41`) provide high statutory clarity under Law `14.478/2022`, but introduce substantial commercial entry barriers. Resolution `520/2025` classifies cross-border stablecoin transfers as regulated Foreign Exchange (`Cambio`) operations, restricts transactions with unhosted wallets, and requires `SPSAV` authorization with minimum paid-in capital between `BRL 2 million and BRL 14 million+` (`~USD 350,000-2.5M`). Combined with `BCB Pix` ubiquity (`over 175M` users, `REF-42`), these rules price out uncapitalised ecosystem pilots.
- **Andean (`Colombia, Peru` - `Monitor - 2.80 / 5.00`, `RARF D4 = 3`):** Colombia remains in a statutory VASP licensing vacuum following the conclusion of the Financial Superintendency's (`SFC`) `LaArenera` pilot sandbox (`REF-46`), leaving virtual-asset operators exposed to sudden commercial-bank account closures until Congress enacts the pending `PSAV` bill. Peru's SBS Resolution `02648-2024` (effective August 2024, `REF-48`) establishes an AML and `USD 1,000` Travel Rule registry with `UIF-Peru`, but does not create a prudential payment-licensing perimeter.
- **Mexico (`Monitor - 2.75 / 5.00`, `RARF D4 = 2`):** Under `Banxico Circular 4/2019` (`REF-44`), regulated Mexican financial technology institutions (`ITFs` under `Ley Fintech 2018`) and commercial banks are barred from offering client-facing virtual-asset custody or exchange on regulated balance sheets, restricting virtual-asset use to prior-authorized internal back-office operations. Operators such as `Bitso` (`REF-45`) must maintain bifurcated corporate structures pairing a regulated `IFPE` (`SPEI` fiat leg) with a separate `LFPIORPI Art. 17 Section XVI` entity (`virtual-asset leg`).
- **Horn / Ethiopia (`No-Go - 2.05 / 5.00`, `RARF D4 = 2`):** The National Bank of Ethiopia (`NBE`) Public Notice of May 2022 and National Payment System (Amendment) Proclamation `No. 1282/2023` (`REF-26`) prohibit cryptocurrency payments and virtual-asset settlement, while Foreign Exchange Directive `FXD/01/2024` (`REF-27`) restricts formal remittance termination to NBE-licensed banks and `Telebirr`/`EthSwitch` (`REF-30`). In parallel, Digital Identification Proclamation `No. 1284/2023` (`Fayda MOSIP`, `REF-28`), Personal Data Protection Proclamation `No. 1321/2024`, and `INSA` Proclamation `No. 808/2013` (`REF-29`) mandate sovereign local server hosting and federal security clearance for public cryptographic systems.
